# From reading to explaining

RabbitHole addresses a practical gap: research can feel familiar while the relationships remain difficult to reconstruct. Automatic collection reduces the need to upload everything again. A map externalizes the relationships while keeping the actual sources nearby. The map is not proof of understanding, and Go's inference is labeled separately from direct support.

Explain the connection is the centerpiece. A user sees a relationship question, writes an explanation from memory, then reveals Go's saved model explanation and the captured evidence. They decide whether to revisit it. That action asks for retrieval before rereading. A rough explanation can expose a missing mechanism or an unsupported assumption. Fresh personalized critique is available through the explicit Go handoff; the UI does not pretend a self-rating is AI feedback.

Flashcards focus on one compact recall question. A short multiple-choice quiz offers deterministic choice checking and a source-backed explanation. Attempts persist with the analysis version. Again/understood is simple progress tracking, not a validated spaced-repetition scheduler. The product does not claim better grades, retention improvements, or measured learning gains: these are intended benefits to investigate, not results established by this build.

Quality review before the demo: choose a real edge, read its evidence, confirm the verb means something, try the prompt without the answer, and verify the model answer does not add unsupported causal claims. If the evidence is thin, a gap and useful next search are better than a confident invented connection.
